# Elements of Style — Composition Principles

Core rules from Strunk's *The Elements of Style* for clear, forceful writing.

## Elementary Rules of Usage

1. **Form possessive singular by adding 's.** Charles's friend, Burns's poems.
2. **Use comma after each term in a series except the last.** Red, white, and blue.
3. **Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas.** The best way to see a country, unless you are pressed for time, is to travel on foot.
4. **Comma before conjunction introducing co-ordinate clause.** The situation is perilous, but there is still one chance of escape.
5. **Don't join independent clauses with a comma.** Use a semicolon, or split into two sentences.
6. **Don't break sentences in two.** Don't use periods for commas.
7. **Participial phrase at beginning refers to grammatical subject.** "Walking slowly down the road, he saw a woman..." — "he" must be walking.

## Elementary Principles of Composition

### Rule 8: One paragraph per topic
Each paragraph covers one idea. The beginning of each paragraph signals a new step in the development of the subject.

### Rule 9: Begin with topic sentence, end in conformity
The topic sentence comes at or near the beginning. Succeeding sentences explain or develop it. The final sentence emphasizes the thought or states an important consequence. Avoid ending with a digression.

### Rule 10: Use the active voice

| Weak (Passive) | Strong (Active) |
|---|---|
| There were a great number of dead leaves lying on the ground. | Dead leaves covered the ground. |
| The sound of a guitar somewhere in the house could be heard. | Somewhere in the house a guitar hummed sleepily. |
| The reason that he left college was that his health became impaired. | Failing health compelled him to leave college. |
| It was not long before he was very sorry that he had said what he had. | He soon repented his words. |
| A survey of this region was made in 1900. | This region was surveyed in 1900. |

Use passive when the receiver of the action is the topic: "The dramatists of the Restoration are little esteemed to-day." Avoid chaining one passive upon another.

### Rule 11: Put statements in positive form

Make definite assertions. Use *not* for denial or antithesis, never evasion.

| Weak (Negative) | Strong (Positive) |
|---|---|
| not honest | dishonest |
| not important | trifling |
| did not remember | forgot |
| did not pay any attention to | ignored |
| did not have much confidence in | distrusted |

The antithesis of negative and positive is strong: "Not charity, but simple justice."

### Rule 12: Use definite, specific, concrete language

Prefer the specific to the general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| A period of unfavorable weather set in. | It rained every day for a week. |
| He showed satisfaction as he took possession of his well-earned reward. | He grinned as he pocketed the coin. |

The surest method of arousing and holding attention is by being specific, definite, and concrete.

### Rule 13: Omit needless words

A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences. Make every word tell.

| Wordy | Concise |
|---|---|
| the question as to whether | whether |
| there is no doubt but that | no doubt / doubtless |
| used for fuel purposes | used for fuel |
| he is a man who | he |
| in a hasty manner | hastily |
| this is a subject which | this subject |
| owing to the fact that | since / because |
| in spite of the fact that | though / although |
| call your attention to the fact that | remind you |

Eliminate "who is" and "which was" when superfluous: "His brother, who is a member of the same firm" → "His brother, a member of the same firm."

Combine step-by-step presentation of a single complex idea into one sentence:

| Wordy (51 words) | Concise (26 words) |
|---|---|
| Macbeth was very ambitious. This led him to wish to become king of Scotland. The witches told him that this wish of his would come true... | Encouraged by his wife, Macbeth achieved his ambition and realized the prediction of the witches by murdering Duncan and becoming king of Scotland in his place. |

### Rule 14: Avoid succession of loose sentences
Don't chain clauses with "and", "but", "so", "who", "which" repeatedly. Vary with simple sentences, semicolons, and periodic sentences.

### Rule 15: Express coordinate ideas in similar form (parallel structure)

| Broken Parallel | Parallel |
|---|---|
| Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method, while now the laboratory method is employed. | Formerly, science was taught by the textbook method; now it is taught by the laboratory method. |
| It was both a long ceremony and very tedious. | The ceremony was both long and tedious. |
| A time not for words, but action. | A time not for words, but for action. |

### Rule 16: Keep related words together

Don't separate subject and verb with transferable phrases. Place relative pronouns immediately after antecedents. Place modifiers next to what they modify.

| Separated | Together |
|---|---|
| Wordsworth, in the fifth book of The Excursion, gives a minute description of this church. | In the fifth book of The Excursion, Wordsworth gives a minute description of this church. |
| He only found two mistakes. | He found only two mistakes. |

### Rule 17: In summaries, keep to one tense
Use present tense for drama summaries, present or past for stories. Don't shift between tenses.

### Rule 18: Place emphatic words at end of sentence

| Weak Ending | Strong Ending |
|---|---|
| Humanity has hardly advanced in fortitude since that time, though it has advanced in many other ways. | Humanity, since that time, has advanced in many other ways, but it has hardly advanced in fortitude. |
| This steel is principally used for making razors, because of its hardness. | Because of its hardness, this steel is principally used in making razors. |

The beginning of a sentence is also a prominent position. The end carries the most weight.

## Common Word Misuses

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "And/or" | Choose one or rewrite |
| "As to whether" | Just "whether" |
| "Case" (padding) | Remove it: "in many cases" → "often" |
| "Certainly" (overuse) | Cut or replace with evidence |
| "Clever" (misused as good) | Use for ingenuity only |
| "Comprise" | The whole comprises the parts, not vice versa |
| "Currently" (usually needless) | Cut it |
| "Data" | Plural: "data are" |
| "Due to" | Use "owing to" or "because of" |
| "Each and every one" | Just "each" or "every" |
| "Interesting" (says nothing) | State why it's interesting |
| "Kind of / sort of" | Avoid as hedging; use for classification only |
| "Literally" | Only when actually literal |
| "Nice" (too vague) | Be specific about what quality |
| "One of the most" | Empty superlative — be specific |
| "Very" (weakens) | Replace with a stronger word or cut |
