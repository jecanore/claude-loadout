# Content Templates

Structure and guidelines for each major content type.

## Blog Post

### Structure
1. **Headline** — benefit-driven, includes primary keyword, <60 chars for SEO
2. **Introduction** (100-150 words) — hook with question, stat, bold claim, or scenario. State what the post covers. Include primary keyword.
3. **Body sections** (3-5) — each with descriptive H2 subheading. Use H3 for subsections. One core idea per section with evidence, examples, or data.
4. **Conclusion** (75-100 words) — summarize key takeaways, reinforce main message, include CTA.
5. **Meta description** — <160 chars, includes primary keyword, compels the click.

### Best Practices
- 8th-grade reading level for broad audiences; adjust up for technical
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
- Subheadings every 200-300 words
- Bullet points and numbered lists to break up text
- At least one data point, example, or quote per section
- Active voice, front-load key information
- Internal links to 2-3 related posts

## Landing Page

### Structure
1. **Headline** — primary benefit in <10 words
2. **Subheadline** — elaborates with supporting context
3. **Hero section** — headline + subheadline + primary CTA + supporting image/video
4. **Value propositions** — 3-4 benefit-driven sections with icons or images
5. **Social proof** — testimonials, logos, stats, case study snippets
6. **Objection handling** — FAQ or trust signals
7. **Final CTA** — repeat the primary call to action

### Best Practices
- Lead with benefits, not features
- Use "you" language — speak to the reader directly
- Minimize jargon unless audience expects it
- Every section answers "so what?" from reader's perspective
- Reduce friction: fewer form fields, clear next steps, trust signals near CTAs

## Social Media

### Platform-Specific Guidelines

**LinkedIn** (professional but human)
- 1,300 characters is the sweet spot before "see more"
- Personal stories and lessons perform well
- Paragraph breaks for readability
- 3-5 relevant hashtags

**Twitter/X** (concise and punchy)
- Strong opening words — first line must hook
- Threads for longer narratives
- Engage with replies
- 1-3 hashtags max

**Instagram** (visual-first)
- Storytelling hooks in captions
- Line breaks for readability
- Hashtags in first comment or at end
- 5-15 relevant hashtags

**Facebook** (conversational)
- Questions drive comments
- Shorter posts (<80 chars) get more engagement for links
- Conversational tone

### Universal Social Structure
- **Hook** — first line grabs attention (question, bold statement, number)
- **Body** — 2-4 concise points or short narrative
- **CTA** — what should reader do (comment, click, share, tag)
- **Hashtags** — 3-5 relevant (platform-dependent)

## Email Newsletter

### Structure
1. **Subject line** — <50 chars, creates curiosity or states clear value
2. **Preview text** — complements subject line, does not repeat it
3. **Header/hero** — visual anchor + one-line value statement
4. **Body sections** — 2-3 content blocks, scannable with bold intro sentences
5. **Primary CTA** — one clear action per email, visually distinct
6. **Footer** — unsubscribe link, company info, social links

### Best Practices
- Write subject lines with urgency, curiosity, or clear value
- Personalize where possible (name, company, behavior)
- One primary CTA per email
- Scannable: bold key phrases, short paragraphs, bullet points
- Mobile-first: most email is read on mobile
- Test everything: subject lines, send times, CTA copy, layout

## Case Study

### Structure
1. **Title** — "[Customer] achieves [result] with [product]"
2. **Snapshot** — customer name, industry, company size, product used, key result (sidebar/callout)
3. **Challenge** — what problem the customer faced
4. **Solution** — what was implemented and how
5. **Results** — quantified outcomes with specific metrics
6. **Quote** — customer testimonial
7. **CTA** — learn more, get a demo, read more case studies

### Best Practices
- Lead with the result in the title
- Use specific numbers: "reduced churn by 23%" not "significantly reduced churn"
- Include a direct customer quote
- Keep it scannable — use headers, bullet points, callout boxes

## Press Release

### Structure
1. **Headline** — factual, newsworthy, <80 chars
2. **Subheadline** — optional, adds context
3. **Dateline** — city, state, date
4. **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2-3 sentences
5. **Body paragraphs** — supporting details, quotes, context
6. **Boilerplate** — company description (standardized)
7. **Media contact** — name, email, phone

### Best Practices
- Inverted pyramid: most important info first
- One newsworthy angle per release
- Include a quote from a company leader
- Keep to one page (400-600 words)
- No promotional language — factual and objective

## Content Length Guidelines

| Type | Target Length | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Blog post (standard) | 1,200-1,800 words | Covers topic thoroughly |
| Blog post (pillar) | 3,000-5,000 words | Comprehensive reference |
| Landing page | 500-1,500 words | Depends on offer complexity |
| Social media post | Platform-specific | See guidelines above |
| Email newsletter | 200-500 words body | Scannable, one CTA |
| Case study | 800-1,200 words | Results-focused |
| Press release | 400-600 words | One page |
